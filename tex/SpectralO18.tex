  \begin{abstract}
    The identification of conjugate Weil blocks $\{c,\, q-c\}$ as the fibres of the
    non-injective projection $\Proj:\chifield\to\Obs$ was stated as a structurally motivated
    hypothesis in O16~\cite{Beau2026a20} and used in O17~\cite{Beau2026a21} to derive the
    pair-level observable and the exponent doubling $\dpair = 2\,\delta_{c} \approx 7.44$.
    The present paper provides the foundational derivation of the underlying involution.
    We proceed in two stages.
    At the abstract level, we show that the Born--Infeld action $S[\chifield]$ is even in
    $\chifield$, and we define a notion of \emph{BI-indiscernability} for configurations that
    produce identical effective responses under all BI-admissible perturbations.
    Evenness of $S$ immediately implies that $\chifield$ and $-\chifield$ are BI-indiscernible,
    so every projective fibre contains the orbit $\{\chifield, -\chifield\}$.
    We then prove a conditional minimality result: in the absence of any symmetry of
    $S[\chifield]$ beyond its parity, the minimal fibre is exactly the involution
    $\chifield\mapsto-\chifield$.
    At the concrete level, the O17 result $\rhoqc = \overline{\rhoc}$ together with the
    norm-invariance of Gram--Schmidt orthogonalisation identifies the involution
    $c\leftrightarrow q-c$ as the Weil-representation instance of the abstract parity.
    This closes the logical gap in O16--O17 by deriving the fibre structure from the
    Born--Infeld parity rather than postulating it.
  \end{abstract}

  \textbf{Keywords.}
  fibre-level admissibility, Born--Infeld parity, BI-indiscernability, parity involution,
  non-injective projection, Weil representation, Heisenberg Cayley graphs, pair observable,

  \section{Context and scope}
    \label{sec:context}

    The Cosmochrony framework~\cite{Cosmochrony} describes physics as emerging from a
    pre-geometric substrate field $\chifield$ projected onto effective observables via a map
    $\Proj:\chifield\to\Obs$.
    A central structural motivation of the framework, developed in the companion
    paper~\cite{Beau2026c}, is that $\Proj$ is non-injective: injectivity would imply trivial
    holonomy and would therefore preclude any emergent gauge structure.
    Finite projective capacity is the capacity axiom [A-cap] of~\cite{Beau2026c}, motivated by
    non-injective projection with finite local distinguishability, and the Born--Infeld
    completion of the effective action is the saturation candidate compatible with this
    axiom~\cite{Beau2026c}.

    The O-series spectral admissibility sub-programme has investigated the capacity exponent
    $\delta$ governing projective dynamics on Heisenberg Cayley graphs $\Cay(G_q,S_q)$ with
    $G_q = \Heis$ for prime $q$.
    Papers O12--O15~\cite{Beau2026a16,Beau2026a17,Beau2026a18,Beau2026a19} established the
    exact block-level exponent $\hat{\delta}_{\mathrm{exact}} \approx 3.72$, structurally
    below the phenomenological target $\delta \in [7.4, 10.6]$.
    O16~\cite{Beau2026a20} identified the resolution: the physically relevant observable
    lives at the level of conjugate pairs $\{c, q-c\}$, yielding $\dpair = 2\,\delta_c
  \approx 7.44$.
    O17~\cite{Beau2026a21} provided the structural derivation showing that the Gram--Schmidt
    dynamics of blocks $c$ and $q-c$ are identical, establishing $\delta_{q-c} = \delta_c$
    and that the factor $r(c,q)$ in O16 is a normalisation artefact, not an invariant.

    However, O17 Section~5.2 explicitly identifies as the primary open problem of the
    fibre-level programme the derivation of the identification $c \leftrightarrow q-c$ as a
    necessary consequence of the projection structure $\Proj$ and the Born--Infeld constraint,
    rather than a structurally motivated hypothesis.
    The present paper addresses this open problem.

    \paragraph{Scope and what this paper does not claim.}
      O18 establishes three things:
      (i) the Born--Infeld action is even in $\chifield$;
      (ii) this evenness forces the projective fibres to contain the involution
      $\chifield\mapsto-\chifield$;
      (iii) in the Weil realisation of $\Heis$, this involution is concretely
      $c\leftrightarrow q-c$.
      O18 does \emph{not} claim that the involution is the unique possible fibre structure in
      all enriched frameworks: the minimality result is explicitly conditional on the absence of
      additional symmetries beyond Born--Infeld parity (Section~\ref{sec:abstract}).
      A general classification of all possible fibres is deferred to a subsequent paper.

  \section{Abstract level: parity from Born--Infeld}
  \label{sec:abstract}

  \subsection{Imported results on non-injectivity}
    \label{ssec:noninjectivity}

    We recall two structural inputs from the companion paper~\cite{Beau2026c} that are used
    here without re-derivation.

    \begin{enumerate}
      \item \textbf{Non-injectivity of $\Proj$}~\cite{Beau2026c}.
      If $\Proj$ were injective and compatible with relational locality, the induced holonomy
      would be trivial, which would preclude emergent gauge structure.
      Since electromagnetism must arise as an effective U(1) fibre symmetry, this argument
      motivates taking $\Proj$ to be non-injective.
      Non-trivial fibres $\Proj^{-1}(y)$ are therefore structurally motivated rather than an
      arbitrary auxiliary assumption.

      \item \textbf{Capacity axiom and Born--Infeld candidate}~\cite{Beau2026c}.
      Finite projective transport capacity is the capacity axiom [A-cap] of~\cite{Beau2026c},
      an explicit hypothesis motivated by non-injective projection with finite local
      distinguishability.
      The Born--Infeld effective action is the saturation candidate compatible with this
      axiom, analytic in the weak-field limit and saturating the constitutive relation; it is
      the form adopted throughout this paper.
      The effective action takes the form
      \begin{equation}
        S_{\mathrm{BI}}[\chifield]
        = \beta^{2} \int \!\mathrm{d}^{4}x
        \left(1 - \sqrt{1 - \frac{F^{2}}{\beta^{2}}}\right),
        \qquad F = D\chifield,
        \label{eq:SBI}
      \end{equation}
      where $\beta$ is the saturation scale and $F^{2} = F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}$.
    \end{enumerate}

  \subsection{Evenness of the Born--Infeld action}
    \label{ssec:parity}

    \begin{lemma}[Parity of $S_{\mathrm{BI}}$]
      \label{lem:parity}
      $S_{\mathrm{BI}}[-\chifield] = S_{\mathrm{BI}}[\chifield]$.
    \end{lemma}

    \begin{proof}
      Under $\chifield \mapsto -\chifield$, the field gradient transforms as
      $F = D\chifield \mapsto -D\chifield = -F$.
      Therefore $F^{2} = F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu} \mapsto (-F)_{\mu\nu}(-F)^{\mu\nu} = F^{2}$.
      Since the integrand in~\eqref{eq:SBI} depends on $\chifield$ only through $F^{2}$,
      the action is unchanged.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{remark}
      Lemma~\ref{lem:parity} uses only the quadratic dependence on $F$ in the argument of the
      square root, which is the defining property of the Born--Infeld structure.
      It does not depend on any discrete group structure and holds for all smooth $\chifield$.
    \end{remark}

  \subsection{BI-indiscernability and the fibre constraint}
    \label{ssec:indiscern}

    \begin{definition}[BI-indiscernability]
      \label{def:indiscern}
      Two configurations $\chifield_{1}, \chifield_{2}$ are \emph{BI-indiscernible} if, for
      every BI-admissible perturbation $\eta$ (i.e.\ $\|D\eta\|$ bounded and
      $\|D(\chifield_{i} + \epsilon\eta)\| \leq \cchi$ for small $\epsilon > 0$), every
      effective response functional $R$ constructed solely from $S_{\mathrm{BI}}$ and its
      derivatives --- in particular, $R$ depends on $\chifield$ only through BI-admissible local
      invariants, hence only through $F^{2} = (D\chifield)^{2}$ and its covariant derivatives,
      with no residual orientation-sensitive dependence (i.e.\ responses invariant under
      BI-admissible perturbations at fixed resolution scale) --- satisfies
      \begin{equation}
        R[\chifield_{1} + \epsilon\eta]
        = R[\chifield_{2} + \epsilon\eta]
        \quad\text{for all admissible } \eta \text{ and all sufficiently small } \epsilon.
        \label{eq:indiscern}
      \end{equation}
    \end{definition}

    \begin{corollary}[Involution is contained in every fibre]
      \label{cor:involution}
      For every $\chifield$, the pair $\{\chifield, -\chifield\}$ consists of BI-indiscernible
      configurations.
      Consequently, every projective fibre $\Proj^{-1}(y)$ contains the orbit
      $\{\chifield, -\chifield\}$ for each $\chifield \in \Proj^{-1}(y)$.
    \end{corollary}

    \begin{proof}
      By Lemma~\ref{lem:parity}, $S_{\mathrm{BI}}[\chifield] = S_{\mathrm{BI}}[-\chifield]$.
      Any response functional $R$ built from $S_{\mathrm{BI}}$ and BI-admissible perturbations
      therefore satisfies $R[-\chifield + \epsilon\eta] = R[\chifield + \epsilon(-\eta)]$.
      The admissibility condition in Definition~\ref{def:indiscern} requires $\|D\eta\|$ bounded
      and $\|D(\chifield_{i}+\epsilon\eta)\|\leq\cchi$; replacing $\eta$ by $-\eta$ preserves
      both bounds, so the admissible set is closed under $\eta\mapsto-\eta$.
      Consequently, since $-\eta$ ranges over all admissible perturbations whenever $\eta$ does,
      the response of $\chifield$ under $\eta$ equals the response of $-\chifield$ under $-\eta$,
      and hence also under $\eta$.
      This yields $R[\chifield + \epsilon\eta] = R[-\chifield + \epsilon\eta]$ for all admissible
      $\eta$, so $\chifield$ and $-\chifield$ are BI-indiscernible by
      Definition~\ref{def:indiscern}.
    \end{proof}

  \subsection{Minimality of the involution (conditional result)}
    \label{ssec:minimality}

    Corollary~\ref{cor:involution} shows that the parity orbit is \emph{contained} in every
    fibre.
    We now address whether the parity orbit is the \emph{minimal} fibre, i.e.\ whether any
    additional identification $\chifield \sim \psi$ with $\psi \neq -\chifield$ is forced by
    the Born--Infeld structure.

    \begin{theorem}[Conditional minimality of the parity fibre]
      \label{thm:minimality}
      Suppose the only global effective symmetry of $S_{\mathrm{BI}}[\chifield]$ compatible
      with BI-admissible observables is the parity $\chifield \mapsto -\chifield$.
      Then the minimal BI-indiscernible fibre is the two-element orbit
      $\{\chifield, -\chifield\}$.
      Any identification $\chifield \sim \psi$ with $\psi \neq \pm\chifield$ either
      \begin{enumerate}
        \item[(i)] implies the existence of a further effective symmetry of $S_{\mathrm{BI}}$
        compatible with BI-admissible observables beyond parity, contradicting the hypothesis; or
        \item[(ii)] is unstable, in the sense that there exists a BI-admissible perturbation
        $\eta$ for which
        $R[\chifield + \epsilon\eta] \neq R[\psi + \epsilon\eta]$
        for some response functional $R$.
      \end{enumerate}
    \end{theorem}

    \begin{proof}
      Suppose $\chifield \sim \psi$ with $\psi \neq \pm\chifield$, i.e.\
      $R[\chifield + \epsilon\eta] = R[\psi + \epsilon\eta]$ for all admissible $\eta$ and
      all $R$.
      Setting $\epsilon = 0$, this requires $R[\chifield] = R[\psi]$ for all $R$, hence
      $S_{\mathrm{BI}}[\chifield] = S_{\mathrm{BI}}[\psi]$.
      The map $\chifield \mapsto \psi$ therefore induces an invariance of all BI-derived
      observables, hence an effective symmetry of the action $S_{\mathrm{BI}}$.
      By hypothesis, the only such effective symmetry is parity, so $\psi = -\chifield$, a
      contradiction.
      If instead~\eqref{eq:indiscern} holds at $\epsilon = 0$ but fails for some admissible
      $\eta$ at positive $\epsilon$, then case~(ii) applies by definition.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{remark}[Status of the hypothesis]
      \label{rem:conditionality}
      The hypothesis that parity is the only global effective symmetry of $S_{\mathrm{BI}}$
      compatible with BI-admissible observables is not proved here; it is a structural
      assumption about the effective action.
      Theorem~\ref{thm:minimality} should therefore be read as: \emph{given the
      Born--Infeld structure alone, the minimal fibre is the parity orbit.}
      If the effective action admits additional symmetries in an enriched framework
      (for instance, from additional topological or gauge constraints), the fibre could
      be larger.
      A classification of fibres in such enriched settings is deferred to a subsequent paper.
    \end{remark}

    The abstract-level conclusion is:

    \begin{theorem}[Abstract parity involution]
      \label{thm:abstract}
      Under the Born--Infeld effective action~\eqref{eq:SBI} and in the absence of global
      effective symmetries of $S_{\mathrm{BI}}$ beyond parity, the projection $\Proj$ has
      minimal fibres given by the involution
      \begin{equation}
        \Proj(\chifield) = \Proj(-\chifield)
        \qquad\text{for all } \chifield \in \mathcal{C}_{\chi},
        \label{eq:abstract_involution}
      \end{equation}
      i.e.\ $\Proj$ factors through the $\mathbb{Z}_{2}$-orbit $\{\chifield, -\chifield\}$.
    \end{theorem}

  \section{Concrete level: Weil realisation in $\Heis$}
  \label{sec:concrete}

  \subsection{The setting}
    \label{ssec:setting}

    We work in the discrete Heisenberg group $G_q = \Heis$ for prime $q$, with the Cayley
    graph $\Cay(G_q, S_q)$ where $S_q = \{X^{\pm 1}, Y^{\pm 1}\}$ and $X, Y$ are the
    standard generators.
    The Weil representations $\rhoc$ of $G_q$ are parametrised by the character index
    $c \in (\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^{\times}$.
    The Gram--Schmidt redundancy $\sigma_c(n)$ at BFS shell $n$ is the key spectral
    observable established in O12--O15~\cite{Beau2026a16,Beau2026a17,Beau2026a18,Beau2026a19}.

  \subsection{Imported result from O17}
    \label{ssec:O17}

    The following result is Theorem~2.1 and Corollary~2.3 of O17~\cite{Beau2026a21}.

    \begin{theorem}[O17 Theorem 2.1 and Corollary 2.3]
      \label{thm:O17}
      The conjugation identity $\rhoqc = \overline{\rhoc}$ together with the norm-invariance of
      the Gram--Schmidt process implies that the raw Gram--Schmidt redundancy counts are
      exactly identical for $\rhoc$ and $\rhoqc$ when the initial supports coincide, and
      structurally isomorphic in general.
      In particular, $\delta_{q-c} = \delta_c$ for all admissible $c$.
    \end{theorem}

  \subsection{Identification of the abstract involution}
    \label{ssec:identification}

    Theorem~\ref{thm:abstract} establishes that the Born--Infeld structure forces an
    involution $\Proj(\chifield) = \Proj(-\chifield)$.
    We now identify the concrete realisation of this involution in the Weil setting.

    The conjugation $\rhoqc = \overline{\rhoc}$ is a complex conjugation of representations.
    Complex conjugation is the natural realisation of a sign inversion at the level of
    representation amplitudes: if a representation $\rhoc$ encodes the response of the
    substrate field $\chifield$ at character $c$, then $\overline{\rhoc}$ encodes the
    response of $-\chifield$ at the same character, since complex conjugation reverses the
    phase of the effective observable.
    The index shift $c \mapsto q-c$ is therefore the concrete group-theoretic realisation of
    the abstract sign inversion $\chifield \mapsto -\chifield$ within $G_q$.

    This identification is consistent with, and independently supported by,
    Theorem~\ref{thm:O17}: the dynamical indistinguishability of blocks $c$ and $q-c$ under
    Gram--Schmidt is precisely the spectral manifestation of BI-indiscernability
    (Definition~\ref{def:indiscern}) in the Weil setting.

    \begin{remark}[Representation-level identification]
      \label{rem:identification}
      The correspondence $\chifield \leftrightarrow c$, $-\chifield \leftrightarrow q-c$ should
      be understood as a representation-level realisation of the abstract involution: the
      conjugation $\rhoqc = \overline{\rhoc}$ is the concrete spectral instance of the sign
      inversion $\chifield\mapsto-\chifield$ within the Weil setting.
      A direct derivation of this correspondence from $\chifield$-space variables, bypassing the
      representation-theoretic language, is left for future work.
    \end{remark}

    \begin{theorem}[Weil-level parity involution]
      \label{thm:weil}
      In the Weil realisation of $\Heis$, the abstract parity involution
      $\Proj(\chifield) = \Proj(-\chifield)$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:abstract} is concretely
      realised as
      \begin{equation}
        \Proj(c) = \Proj(q - c)
        \qquad\text{for all } c \in (\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^{\times}.
        \label{eq:weil_involution}
      \end{equation}
      The conjugate pair $\{c, q-c\}$ is the minimal fibre of $\Proj$ in the Weil setting,
      conditional on the absence of additional symmetries of $S_{\mathrm{BI}}$ beyond parity.
    \end{theorem}

    \begin{proof}
      By Theorem~\ref{thm:abstract}, every fibre of $\Proj$ contains the orbit
      $\{\chifield, -\chifield\}$.
      The identification $\chifield \leftrightarrow c$ and $-\chifield \leftrightarrow q-c$
      follows from the complex conjugation $\rhoqc = \overline{\rhoc}$, which is the spectral
      realisation of $\chifield \mapsto -\chifield$ in the Weil setting (Section~\ref{ssec:identification}).
      The minimality is inherited from Theorem~\ref{thm:minimality} under the same conditional
      hypothesis.
    \end{proof}

  \section{Implications and relation to O16--O17}
  \label{sec:implications}

  \subsection{Requalification of O16 and O17}
    \label{ssec:requalification}

    O16~\cite{Beau2026a20} introduced the pair observable $\sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}(n) =
  \sigma_c(n) \cdot \sigma_{q-c}(n)$ and stated the identification of $\{c, q-c\}$ with
    fibres of $\Proj$ as a structurally motivated hypothesis (O16 Remark~6.2).
    O17~\cite{Beau2026a21} derived the equality $\delta_{q-c} = \delta_c$ from
    $\rhoqc = \overline{\rhoc}$ and identified $r(c,q)$ as a normalisation artefact,
    but left the fibre identification as the primary open problem (O17 Section~5.2).
    Theorem~\ref{thm:weil} closes this open problem: the identification is now a derived
    consequence of the Born--Infeld structure and is no longer a hypothesis.

    \begin{remark}[Complementarity with O15--O17]
O15~\cite{Beau2026a19} established that no reweighting of block-level data can recover
the fibre-level exponent.
O16--O17 established the correct observable and exponent at the pair level.
O18 (the present paper) provides the foundational derivation of the fibre structure
underlying O16--O17.
The three results are complementary: O15 closes the block-level programme; O16--O17 open
and populate the fibre-level programme; O18 provides its structural foundation.
    \end{remark}

  \subsection{Effect on $\delta_{\mathrm{pair}} \to \bstar$}
    \label{ssec:dbetastar}

    The structural relation $\bstar = 1/(\delta + 1/2)$, used throughout the O-series, should
    be evaluated at $\delta = \dpair \approx 7.44$ as established in O16--O17.
    This evaluation was already performed in O17 Section~4 on the basis of the (then
    hypothetical) fibre identification.
    Theorem~\ref{thm:weil} removes the hypothetical status: the substitution
    $\delta \to \dpair$ is now a derived consequence of the fibre structure, not an
    assumption.
    This also removes the last remaining observable-level ambiguity identified in O15:
    the aggregation no-go of O15 Corollary~4.8~\cite{Beau2026a19} showed that no
    block-level reweighting recovers the correct exponent; O18 now explains why the fibre
    level is the structurally mandated one.
    The full derivation of the $\dpair \to \bstar$ relation at the fibre level, without
    reliance on pipeline conventions, is the subject of O21.

  \subsection{Open directions}
    \label{ssec:open}

    The present paper leaves three directions open.

    \textbf{Canonical normalisation.}
    The pipeline normalisation factor $D(c, b_1, b_2, n)$ is currently implicit in the
    O12/O13 implementation.
    Defining a canonical normalisation independent of $(b_1, b_2)$ would eliminate $r$
    entirely from the pair observable, producing a version of $\sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}$ that
    is genuinely independent of the measurement convention.
    This is a technical, not conceptual, task and is the subject of O19.

    \textbf{Upper bound of the phenomenological window.}
    Numerical exploration (post-O16) shows that the spectrum of $\dpair$ has a hard lower
    bound near $7.4$ and extends above $10.6$.
    The mechanism selecting $[7.4, 10.6]$ as the physical sub-spectrum is not yet identified.
    It appears to be a dynamical persistence criterion rather than a spectral one, and is the
    subject of O20.

    \textbf{General classification of fibres.}
    Theorem~\ref{thm:minimality} is conditional on the absence of additional symmetries.
    A full classification of fibres in frameworks with additional gauge or topological
    structure would determine whether larger fibres are possible and, if so, their structure.
    This programme is deferred to a subsequent paper following O21.

  \section{Summary}
  \label{sec:summary}

  \begin{enumerate}
    \item The Born--Infeld action $S_{\mathrm{BI}}[\chifield]$ is even in $\chifield$
    (Lemma~\ref{lem:parity}).
    This follows directly from the dependence of $S_{\mathrm{BI}}$ on $F^2 = (D\chifield)^2$.

    \item Evenness of $S_{\mathrm{BI}}$ implies that every projective fibre of $\Proj$
    contains the parity orbit $\{\chifield, -\chifield\}$
    (Corollary~\ref{cor:involution}).
    This uses the definition of BI-indiscernability (Definition~\ref{def:indiscern}) and
    does not require any group-theoretic input.

    \item Under the assumption that parity is the only global symmetry of $S_{\mathrm{BI}}$,
    the minimal fibre is the involution $\Proj(\chifield) = \Proj(-\chifield)$
    (Theorems~\ref{thm:minimality} and~\ref{thm:abstract}).
    This is a conditional result; see Remark~\ref{rem:conditionality}.

    \item In the Weil realisation of $\Heis$, the abstract involution is identified with
    $c \leftrightarrow q-c$ via the conjugation $\rhoqc = \overline{\rhoc}$ established in
    O17~\cite{Beau2026a21} (Theorem~\ref{thm:weil}).

    \item These results close the primary open problem stated in O17 Section~5.2.
    The pair observable $\sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}(n) = \sigma_c(n)\cdot\sigma_{q-c}(n)$ and
    the exponent doubling $\dpair = 2\,\delta_c \approx 7.44$ are now structurally derived
    rather than hypothetically motivated.
  \end{enumerate}
